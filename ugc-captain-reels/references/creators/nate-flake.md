## Who he is and why he's a reference

- Fiverr Pro-vetted, top-rated UGC creator (5.0 from ~300+ reviews), who says he has made ads for hundreds of brands. Hosts the *UGC 101* podcast and teaches UGC on Fiverr. Former corporate tech partner manager.
- On camera: millennial guy and girl dad. Relatable, trustworthy everyman, not an influencer.
- Platforms he delivers for: TikTok, TikTok Shop affiliate, Instagram/Facebook Reels, YouTube Shorts.
- Signature formats:
  1. **Podcast-style UGC ad.** The product is pitched as if it's a clip from a podcast conversation.
  2. **Male spokesperson / talking head.** Direct-to-camera recommendation with product demo B-roll.

What to borrow: content that sells while reading as a real person talking, not an ad. Every second either earns attention or gets cut.

## Format A: Podcast-style ad

**Look** *(inferred from the format)*: seated medium shot, a visible broadcast mic on a boom arm in frame, optionally headphones, a softly lit background (shelf, LED accent light). It reads as "a clip from a show". Two angles are faked with punch-ins: wide (zoom 1.0) for setup lines, tight (zoom 1.18–1.25) for the key claim or punchline.

**Beat structure, 30–45s:**
| time | beat | notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2s | Hot-take hook mid-thought | Sounds like a podcast clip cut in mid-conversation: "Okay, I need to talk about…", "Unpopular opinion…", "Nobody is talking about…". Hook text overlay restates it. |
| 2–8s | Personal problem/context | The "I'm a dad, I have zero time" kind of relatable setup. |
| 8–25s | Discovery and specifics | What the product is and 2–3 concrete details. B-roll covers each detail. |
| 25–35s | Proof/result | Their own result or a social-proof stat; tight punch-in for the strongest line. |
| last 3–5s | Casual CTA | "If you've been on the fence, link's right there." Label overlay. |

## Format B: Spokesperson / talking head

**Look** *(inferred)*: handheld or tripod selfie at eye level, natural home setting (kitchen, desk, car), product in hand by the second beat.

**Beat structure, 25–40s:** hook (0–2s, product often visible in hand) → problem (2–6s) → demo with B-roll every claim (6–25s) → result/why it's different (25–32s) → CTA.

## Pacing targets *(inferred; replace with measured numbers)*

- Average shot length: 1.5–3s. Every pause cut out; jump cuts are fine with punch-ins.
- The first cut or visual change lands before 2s.
- Talking speed is brisk and conversational (~160–180 wpm after cuts).
- Final length: 30–45s for podcast style, 20–35s for spokesperson ads.

## Captions and text *(inferred)*

- Bold, centered, 2–3 words at a time with active-word highlight. Use the skill's default caption style.
- One hook text overlay in the first ~2s (≤7 words), plus a CTA label at the end. No other clutter.

## Hook bank (patterns, not scripts)

- Contrarian: "Unpopular opinion: [common belief] is wrong."
- Confession: "I was so wrong about [product category]."
- Overheard/podcast: "We were just talking about this off-mic…"
- Dad POV: "As a dad with [N] kids, this is the only [thing] that…"
- Specific result: "This [product] [specific result] in [timeframe]."
- Callout: "If you [specific pain], stop scrolling."

## Voice

Warm, plain-spoken, slightly self-deprecating. Recommends like a friend, not a salesperson. Claims stay specific and honest, since trust is the whole product.

## Don'ts

- No slow intros, logos, or "hey guys, welcome back".
- No polished commercial voice or stock-footage feel.
- Don't reuse his actual lines. Use the patterns above in the UGC Captain's voice.
